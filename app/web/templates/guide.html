Get your listening stats out of Spotify

Playlist Forge reads the text files that Skiley exports from your Spotify account. It takes about three minutes the first time.

Two sign-ins, neither of them here

You sign in to Spotify on Skiley to export your stats, and later on Soundiiz (or TuneMyMusic) to save a playlist. Both are established third-party tools that use Spotify's official login. Playlist Forge never asks for your Spotify login and never sees it: all it gets is the text files you upload, and it deletes them after 24 hours.

Step by step

  1. Open Skiley's stats page at skiley.net/stats and sign in with Spotify when it asks. {% if "01-skiley-login.png" in shots %}{% endif %}
  2. Pick a list and a time range. Skiley shows your Top Tracks, Top Artists and Top Genres for the last 4 weeks, last 6 months and last 12 months. Each combination is one file, nine in all. {% if "02-skiley-lists.png" in shots %}{% endif %}
  3. Export each list as text. Use Skiley's export (download) button for the list you are looking at. Your browser saves a file such as Top_Tracks_-_Skiley_Export.txt; later downloads of the same list get a number added, like Top_Tracks_-_Skiley_Export__2_.txt. That is fine: Playlist Forge reads the contents, not the file name. {% if "03-skiley-export.png" in shots %}{% endif %}
  4. Repeat for the other lists. Nine files give the best profiles. In a hurry? The three 12-month files (tracks, artists, genres) are enough to start; you can add the rest later.
  5. Upload the files here. Go to Start, select all the files at once, and check the time periods we detected. Then build.

Good to know

  • Skiley's free lists hold up to 50 items; a 4-week list can be shorter if you listened to fewer artists.
  • Only the text files leave Skiley. No playlists are changed, nothing is posted, nothing follows you.
  • If a file does not look like a Skiley export, the upload page says so and nothing is kept.
