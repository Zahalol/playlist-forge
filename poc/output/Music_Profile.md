## The shape of your taste

You are not one listener but about seven, and they take turns. The loudest is a pop-punk and Midwest-emo kid who never left the basement show (28% of your weighted listening, 32% of the recent stuff). Next comes a songwriter-first modern pop era led by Olivia Rodrigo (19%), then a classic-rock, roots and heartland streak that reads like a Milwaukee summer radio dial (16%), and an Americana and alt-country fan who likes songwriters with dirt on their boots (14%, though that one has gone quiet since Dec 2025). Behind them sit a musical-theatre habit that gets binged in bursts (Operation Mincemeat owned your October 2026), an indie and alternative rock layer that connects the others, and a jam-band and bluegrass streak. Hip-hop and EDM are occasional side quests, not a profile. The profiles rarely overlap in genre, but they share a through-line: hooks, real instruments, and singers who sound like they mean it. Two thirds of the artists in your data have under 100k Deezer fans, so you dig deeper than most listeners.

## Method in one paragraph

Every artist in your lists got a recency-weighted taste weight. Profiles were seeded with hand-picked anchor artists and filled in using MusicBrainz genre tags and similarity to the anchors. Recommendations come from ListenBrainz (co-listening) and Deezer (catalogue) similar-artist data, scored by how much of your weighted taste points at each candidate, with anything you already listen to removed. Match % is a relative confidence (5th best = 95%).
